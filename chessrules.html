1. The Chessboard Anatomy & Initial Setup

Chess is played on an 8×8 grid containing 64 alternating light and dark squares. Understanding the grid coordinates and two universal setup rules is essential before making your first move:

Rule 1: "White on Right"

Before setting up pieces, ensure the square in the bottom-right corner for both players (h1 for White, a8 for Black) is a Light (white) square.

Rule 2: "Queen on Her Own Color"

The White Queen always starts on a white square (d1). The Black Queen always starts on a dark square (d8). The King stands right beside her (e1 / e8).

Board Geography: Ranks, Files & Diagonals

  • Ranks (1 to 8): Horizontal rows across the board. White pieces begin on ranks 1 & 2; Black pieces on ranks 7 & 8.
  • Files (a to h): Vertical columns running between the two players.
  • Diagonals: Connected diagonal paths of identical color squares.

Official Initial Game Setup (32 Pieces)

At the start of every chess game, all 32 pieces are arranged symmetrically on the first two and last two ranks:

White starts on ranks 1 & 2 • Black starts on ranks 7 & 8
Starting Roster Breakdown:
  • Back Ranks (Ranks 1 & 8): Rooks on the corners (a & h), Knights on b & g, Bishops on c & f, Queen on d (her color), and King on e.
  • Front Ranks (Ranks 2 & 7): A protective wall of 8 Pawns guarding the officers.
  • First Move: White always moves first, choosing either a pawn move (1 or 2 squares) or a knight leap.

1 Point

Pawns move 1 square straight forward. On their very first move, they have the option to advance 2 squares. Unlike other pieces, pawns capture 1 square diagonally ahead.

3. The Three Special Moves in Chess

Chess features three unique moves designed to add tactical depth and strategic dynamism to the game:

Special Move 1: Castling (0-0 & 0-0-0)

Castling is the only move in chess where two pieces move on the same turn. The King moves two squares toward a Rook, and that Rook hops over to the square the King just crossed. It safeguards the King and brings the Rook into the game.

Click a button on the left to see the King & Rook move simultaneously!
4 Mandatory Rules for Castling:
  • It must be the first move ever for both the King and the chosen Rook.
  • There must be zero intervening pieces between the King and Rook.
  • The King cannot currently be in Check.
  • The King cannot pass through or land on any square attacked by an enemy piece.

Special Move 2: En Passant ("In Passing")

When an opponent advances a pawn two squares forward from its starting rank and lands directly adjacent to your pawn, you may capture it diagonally on the very next move as if it had only moved one square!

Click "Step 1" to start the En Passant demonstration.
  • Strict Time Limit: En Passant must be claimed on the very next turn immediately after the 2-square pawn move, or the right to capture it en passant is lost forever.
  • The captured pawn is removed from the board, and the capturing pawn lands on the square the enemy pawn skipped over.

Special Move 3: Pawn Promotion

When a humble pawn battles its way all the way to the 8th rank (the farthest edge of the board), it immediately transforms into any piece of the player's choice: Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight.

  • No Piece Limit: You can have multiple Queens on the board! There is no requirement to pick a previously captured piece.
  • Over 95% of promotions choose a Queen ("Queening"), though occasionally a Knight is chosen to deliver an immediate fork or checkmate ("Underpromotion").

4. Check, Checkmate & Stalemate

Understanding the exact difference between Check, Checkmate, and Stalemate is the single most important conceptual milestone in chess:

The "CPR" Escape Rule for Checks

When your King is placed in Check, you are under strict legal obligation to get out of Check immediately on that turn. Remember the C-P-R formula:

  • C — Capture: Capture the attacking enemy piece with your King or another friendly piece.
  • P — Protect (Block): Place a friendly piece directly between the checking piece and your King (cannot block Knights or adjacent attacks).
  • R — Run: Move your King to a safe, unattacked adjacent square.
Condition Is King in Check? Any Legal Moves Left? Game Outcome
Check YES YES Game continues. Must escape immediately via CPR.
Checkmate YES NO Game Over! Attacking player wins the game (1 - 0).
Stalemate NO NO Game Over — DRAW! Split points equally (½ - ½).

5. Game Endings & The 5 Ways to Draw

A game of chess does not always conclude with a checkmate. It can conclude in either a decisive result or one of five recognized draw conditions:

1. Mutual Agreement

Both players agree at any point during their turn that neither side has winning prospects.

2. Stalemate

The player whose turn it is has no legal moves and their King is not in check.

3. Threefold Repetition

The exact same board position (with identical legal moves and castling rights) occurs 3 times.

4. 50-Move Rule

50 consecutive moves have been made by both players with zero pawn moves and zero captures.

5. Insufficient Material

Neither player has enough pieces left to mathematically deliver checkmate (e.g., King vs King, King+Bishop vs King, King+Knight vs King).

Resignation & Time

A player may resign at any time if in a hopeless position, or lose if their game clock reaches 00:00.
